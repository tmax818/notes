The ideal scope and sequence for high school chemistry progresses from concrete, observable phenomena to abstract, theoretical concepts.
The American Association of Chemistry Teachers (AACT) organizes its curriculum library into a sequential progression:

* Foundations & Matter

** [[https://teachchemistry.org/classroom-resources/chemistry-basics-unit-plan][Chemistry Basics]]:

SI units, scientific notation, significant figures, data graphing, and classifying/separating matter.

** [[https://teachchemistry.org/classroom-resources/atomic-structure-unit-plan][Atomic Structure]]:

History of atomic theory, subatomic particles, isotopes, and the Bohr model.

* Properties & Structure

** The Periodic Table: Organization, periodic trends (electronegativity, atomic radius).

** Molecules & Bonding: Ionic, covalent, and metallic bonds, Lewis structures, molecular geometry (VSEPR), and intermolecular forces.

** States of Matter: Gas laws and kinetic-molecular theory. 

* Quantitative Chemistry & Reactions

** The Mole & Stoichiometry: Molar mass, Avogadro's number, and balancing equations.

** Reactions & Stoichiometry: Classifying reactions and calculating theoretical yield. 

* Advanced Applications

** Solutions: Molarity, solubility, and colligative properties.

** Acids & Bases: pH scales, titrations, and neutralization.

** Energy & Thermodynamics: Enthalpy, entropy, and specific heat capacity.

** Kinetics & Equilibrium: Reaction rates and Le Chatelier's principle.
